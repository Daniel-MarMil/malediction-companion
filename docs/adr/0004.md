# ADR-04: Server-authoritative match over WebSocket

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-07
- **Revision:** 2026-11-07: shared round, clock and initiative, and handing over the scoreboard (RF-18 and RF-27)
- **Reference:** section 10 of the architecture and requirements document

## Context

Each player changes only their own counters, and the others see them in real time (RF-17). Round, clock and initiative belong to the match, and any player can change them (RF-18), as in Seekers League, the functional reference. Conflicts must be avoided, and any match must be reconstructible.

## Decision

The server is the authority for the match. Each player or team counter has a single owner; round, clock and initiative are shared. Every change is stored in a log of numbered events that the phones receive over WebSocket.

## Rejected alternatives

- CRDTs: unnecessary, because the server orders every change.
- Direct connections between phones: fragile on iOS and Android.

## Consequences

- Simple reconnection: the phone sends its last event number and receives what it missed.
- The history is rebuilt from the events.
- A change of round, clock or initiative carries the value the phone sees; if two arrive at the same time, the server applies the first one and rejects the other.
- The owner can hand over control of their counters to another phone in the match and take it back (RF-27).
